How the counts work

Rumba, Cha-cha, Jive and Bachata music is in 4/4 (four beats to a bar), Samba in 2/4 (two beats). Dancers think in bars, so ballroom tempo is given in bars per minute (MPM).

Rumba: 2, 3, 4-1

Step on 2 and 3 (quick, quick), then step on 4 and stay on that foot through the 1 (slow). The hips finish the action on the 1. You never step on the 1.

Competition: 25–27 MPM · 100–108 BPM

Cha-cha: 2, 3, 4-&-1

Step on 2 and 3, then three quick steps on 4, & and 1: the cha-cha-cha chassé. The & is half a beat, so 4-&-1 takes the same time as two ordinary beats.

Competition: 30–32 MPM · 120–128 BPM

Samba: 1 a 2

Step on 1, a quick step on the a, then step on 2. The a comes a quarter beat before the 2, later and quicker than the cha-cha &. Samba music is in 2/4, so each 1 a 2 is one bar. Here two bars make a group, counted 1 a 2, 3 a 4. Voltas use 1 a 2 a 3 a 4.

Competition: 50–52 MPM · 100–104 BPM

Jive: 1, 2, 3 a 4, 3 a 4

Rock step on 1 and 2, then two chassés, each counted 3 a 4. The a comes a quarter beat before the 4. The basic takes 6 beats and bars have 4, so your 1 meets the music's 1 only every other basic. Pink always marks the music's 1, even when you're counting 3. Use Start my basic here to line up with where you start dancing, and a 3-bar loop to repeat two whole basics.

Competition: 42–44 MPM · 168–176 BPM

Bachata: 1, 2, 3, tap

Step on 1, 2 and 3, then tap on 4 without taking weight, often with a small hip accent. The foot that tapped takes the next step, so you change direction every bar.

Competition (IDO): 28–32 MPM · 112–128 BPM. Many social songs run faster.

Finding the 1 by ear

  • Follow the bass and the chords, not the singer.
  • The chords usually change on the 1.
  • Songs move in groups of 4 or 8 bars. A new section starts on a 1.
  • Singers often start a line just before the 1, so don't count from the first word.
